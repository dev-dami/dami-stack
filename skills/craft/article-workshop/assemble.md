# Assemble

Builds an article in a separate file. The material file is read-only here, so draw on it freely and leave it as it is. Read all of it first, and ask where the article file goes.

Choose the way of building by what the material needs. Say which you picked and why, in a line.

| The material | Way |
|---|---|
| Rich but open, and the path should emerge | Exploratory |
| Points at a claim worth arguing | Thesis |

Both start by agreeing what the reader knows, and both keep the reader ledger from `SKILL.md`.

## Exploratory

The unit is a move: a single step, as long as that step needs. A move that wants several headings is really several moves.

1. Propose two or three ways to open. For each, show what it establishes and what it unlocks.
2. Write only the chosen move, then stop.
3. Re-read the file, then propose two or three continuations the reader is now ready for.
4. Repeat. The piece ends when it is complete, which may be before the material runs out.

Rewrite only a move the user names, in place.

## Thesis

The unit is a block, and every block has to earn its form. The result has one voice.

1. Draft two or three openings that imply different theses. The user chooses or combines them. The opening sets the obligation the piece must meet.
2. Work out what the reader needs at this point, and look for it in the material.
3. Argue the block's form aloud: prose or list or table, inline or aside, quotation or paraphrase.
4. Challenge what the block contributes, and whether it has wandered from the opening.
5. Rework the material into one voice.
6. Name each gap, and ask the user to supply it or cut it.
7. Once a block is agreed, re-read the file and append it straight away. Later changes touch one block where it sits.
8. Repeat until the user says it is finished.

Every paragraph and format choice should be defensible if questioned.

Out of scope in this way: new material from the agent, editing the source file, and platform formatting.
