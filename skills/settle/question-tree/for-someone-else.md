# For someone else

Use when a decision waits on someone other than the user. You write a questionnaire that person can answer alone.

## Ask the user two things

Ask only what the user can always answer, one exchange each.

1. **The recipient:** their role, expertise and relationship to the user. This sets the tone and how much background to give.
2. **What must come back:** the concrete decisions or facts needed.

Aim the questions at the gap between those two.

## Write the file

Write one Markdown file in the current folder. It opens by telling the recipient:

- why this is being asked and what depends on it
- who is asking, and who it is for
- how the answers will be used
- the background an outsider needs
- the deadline and roughly how long it will take
- that partial answers are welcome

Then the questions:

- One idea per question.
- Most important first, since they may read it only once.
- Grouped under themes once there are many.
- Room to answer under each one.
- A reason for asking only where the question could be misread.

End by inviting anything you didn't think to ask.

```markdown
### Refunds

1. How many days after purchase can a customer still ask for a refund?

   Answer:

2. Who may approve a refund above the usual limit?

   Answer:
```

## Finish

Sending it is the user's act. Give them the path and leave it there. When answers come back, use them in the skill `question-tree` or the skill `work-chart`.

## Done when

- Every item that must come back maps to at least one question.
- Each question holds one idea, with the most important first.
- The file holds all six opening items and the closing invitation.
- The file sits in the current folder, its path was given, and nothing was sent.
